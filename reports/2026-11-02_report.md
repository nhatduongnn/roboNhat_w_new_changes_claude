## Short answers

1. **Untrimmed reads and MAPQ.** Yes, the reads are untrimmed (`--no-trim` at basecalling and demux). It does not matter for MAPQ, because the aligner soft-clips the ~117 bp of adapter and barcode and ignores it. Trimming and realigning moved only +10 / −6 of 3,500 random reads across MAPQ 20.
2. **How each A is labelled today.**

   | the A on the read | label | meaning |
   |---|---|---|
   | skipped, not listed in MM | **unmethylated** | p(m6A) < 0.047 |
   | listed, ML 12–48 | **unmethylated** | p ≤ 0.19 |
   | listed, ML 49–206 | **no-call** | ambiguous |
   | listed, ML 207–255 | **methylated** | p ≥ 0.81 |

   Non-A bases are "no observation", not "unmethylated". So your intuition is right: skipped and near-0 As are already called unmethylated. Only the ambiguous middle band is a no-call.
3. **The 10% / 0.81 threshold.** 0.81 is the 10th percentile of *confidence*, max(p, 1−p), over all As. So 90% of As have confidence ≥ 0.81. It is **not** a percentile of ML or of p. On the p scale, 0.81 sits at the 94th percentile of all As and 0.19 at the 84th.
4. **"How often a long chain sits in each state."** Picture the HMM generating an endless chromosome, and count the fraction of bases spent in each state. That fraction is the start probability. The worked example is in §4.
5. **"Entry value" and "path".**
   - The entry value is the probability of jumping from the junction (the central silent state) into a feature.
   - A path is the fixed run of states that feature then walks through: a TF strand = its motif bases in order; the nucleosome = 9 single states, then 128 positions × 4 states, then 10 single states.
   - The entry value is copied along single states and split at the nucleosome's 4-state positions (§4).
6. **End vector in the forward pass.** Not standard. A textbook HMM puts the end weights in one place only. RoboCOP (from upstream) puts them in both the forward and the backward pass, which would count them twice. Ours are all 1, so it has no effect today (§5).
7. **rDNA.**
   - Excluded as its own filter step, before the MAD fence.
   - Background, nucleosome and TF params refit on the new reads: they move < 1.5%.
   - New driver default, ERV46 decoded again.
   - The Nov 3 deck has a new rDNA slide, at the same URL.

## 1. Untrimmed reads and MAPQ
Source: `/usr/project/xtmp/nd141/trim_check/`.

**The reads are untrimmed.**
- Every run's BAM header shows `dorado basecaller sup,6mA … --no-trim` and `dorado demux --no-trim`.
- The rapid-barcode adapter + barcode + flank (~117 bp) sits at the 5' end of 99.2% of mapped reads.

**The aligner soft-clips it.**
- Median 5' soft clip = 117 bp. The clip covers the adapter in 99.15% of reads.
- Clipped bases are not part of the alignment.

**MAPQ measures uniqueness, not length.** minimap2 MAPQ depends on how much better the best hit is than the second-best.
- Correlation of MAPQ with 5' clip length: −0.02.
- Reads with a normal 100–150 bp clip: 3.9% have MAPQ < 20.

**Realignment test (5,000 reads, same aligner and reference).**
- Untrimmed reads reproduce their original MAPQ exactly.
- Trimmed reads:
  - random reads: +10 pass / −6 fail at MAPQ 20;
  - low-MAPQ reads: 120 of 1,500 crossed, but their best and second-best scores stay about equal (ratio ≈ 0.98), so they are still coin-flips between repeat copies.
- Gain: about 0.1–0.3% more reads. Not worth realigning.

**Where the non-rDNA MAPQ < 20 reads sit:** repeats.

86% of them have best ≈ second-best (ratio ≥ 0.9), so they genuinely map to several places.

## 2. Methylated / unmethylated / no-call
Sources: web/spec research, plus a code and data check (`/usr/project/xtmp/nd141/ml_check/`).

**What the tags mean** (SAM spec; dorado 0.9.1 and modkit 0.2.6 source):
- **MM** lists the As that carry a probability (as counts of As to skip between them).
- **ML** holds p(m6A) for each listed A, as integer k = floor(p × 256). k means p is between k/256 and (k+1)/256.
- Our tag is `A+a.`. The `.` means "skipped As have **low** probability of modification", i.e. they are confidently unmethylated.
- dorado only lists As with p ≥ 0.05 (default `--modified-bases-threshold`; ML ≥ 12). Everything below is skipped.
  - In our data, 67.8% of As are skipped and 32.2% listed. The lowest listed ML is 12.
- ML holds p(modified) only. p(unmethylated) = 1 − p.
- So ML is **not** a "quality". It is the probability that this A is methylated:
  - near 0 = confidently unmethylated;
  - near 1 = confidently methylated;
  - near 0.5 = the basecaller can't tell.

**What the code does.** Our reader (`analysis/singlefiber/read_calls.py:203-208`) and modkit agree exactly. Checked on 6,371 positions of chrIV: identical counts (k = 38,046, n = 484,799 on both sides).

| situation | label | share of all As |
|---|---|---|
| skipped A (not listed) | 0 unmethylated (confidence 1) | 67.8% (counted inside the 84.1%) |
| listed, ML 12–48 (p ≤ 0.19) | 0 unmethylated | (inside the 84.1%) |
| **total unmethylated** | | **84.1%** |
| listed, ML 49–206 (0.19 < p < 0.81) | −1 no-call | **9.9%** |
| listed, ML 207–255 (p ≥ 0.81) | 1 methylated | **6.0%** |
| C/G, or a T on a Watson read; mismatches | −1 no observation | — |

**Into the HMM** (`pkgvar/permol_seq_maskoff_logfull_nucbrogsym_filtnordna/robocop/robocop.py:1581-1583`):
- a 1 adds log p;
- a 0 adds log(1 − p);
- a −1 adds 0, i.e. it carries no evidence.

The Bernoulli therefore only ever sees the two confident outcomes. A no-call is dropped, exactly like modkit's `N_fail`.

**Should no-calls be unmethylated instead?** They are As the basecaller couldn't decide on, not As it believes are unmethylated. Pooled linker m6A rate under three rules:

| rule | linker rate |
|---|---|
| current (middle band = no-call) | 0.156 |
| middle band → unmethylated | 0.133 (biased down) |
| split at p = 0.5 (like SMAC-seq) | 0.192 |

- No-calls are 14.5% of linker As vs 9.9% genome-wide, so they are more common in open DNA.
- fibertools uses its own cutoff: it drops m6A calls with ML < 125. Its docs say any hard ML cutoff "will not hold between flow-cells".
- A 3-outcome emission (score the no-calls too) is on the revisit list.

**The threshold** (modkit docs + recomputed from our BAM):
- modkit computes each A's confidence = max(p, 1 − p). Skipped As count as confidence 1.
- It samples reads and takes the **10th percentile** of that confidence: 0.8105469 (= 207.5/256, an ML bin edge).
- **90% of As have confidence ≥ 0.81**; the 10% below become no-calls (we measure 9.9–10.1%).
- A call is methylated if p ≥ 0.81, unmethylated if 1 − p ≥ 0.81 (p ≤ 0.19), otherwise no-call. That is why there are two cutoffs, 0.81 and 0.19.
- 0.81 and 0.19 are *not* the 90th and 10th percentiles of p. Of all As, 94% have p < 0.81 and 84% have p ≤ 0.19.

## 4. Start anywhere, worked example
Full text and numbers: `/usr/project/xtmp/nd141/startend_check/start/worked_example.md`.

**The idea.** Let the HMM generate an endless chromosome and count the fraction of bases spent in each state. A read is a window cut out of that chromosome, so its first base is in state s with exactly that fraction. This is the "stationary distribution". The code computes it in `stationary_initial()`, `_5to3.py:237-268`.

**How the HMM is wired.**
- Every feature starts and ends at one junction (the central silent state).
- From the junction the chain jumps into one of:
  - background, with probability 0.933;
  - a TF strand, with tf_prob/2;
  - the nucleosome, with 0.00136.
- That jump probability is the feature's **entry value**.
- After the jump, the feature walks its **path**: a fixed run of states, one per base, then back to the junction.
  - **Background:** 1 state.
  - **A TF strand:** its motif bases in order, e.g. Abf1 = 14 states forward and 14 reverse.
  - **The nucleosome:** 9 single states, then 128 positions with 4 alternative states each (one per DNA base; the dinucleotide model picks which), then 10 single states. 147 positions in all.

**The rule.**
1. Copy the entry value along every single-state stretch of a path.
2. At a 4-state position, split it according to the transitions into those states. The pieces add back to the entry value.
3. Divide everything by one total.

**Toy example.**
- Junction → background 0.9, TF 0.06 (0.03 per strand, 3 bp motif), nucleosome 0.04.
- The toy nucleosome is N1 → {N2a, N2b} → {N3a, N3b} → N4, with splits 0.3 / 0.7, then 0.6 / 0.4 and 0.2 / 0.8.

| state | how | value |
|---|---|---|
| background | entry | 0.9 |
| each TF base (6 states) | copied | 0.03 |
| N1, N4 | copied | 0.04 |
| N2a / N2b | 0.04 × 0.3 / 0.04 × 0.7 | 0.012 / 0.028 (sum 0.04) |
| N3a / N3b | 0.012×0.6 + 0.028×0.2 / 0.012×0.4 + 0.028×0.8 | 0.0128 / 0.0272 (sum 0.04) |

- Total = 0.9 + 6 × 0.03 + 4 × 0.04 = **1.24**.
- Divide: background 0.726, each TF state 0.0242, N1 and N4 0.0323, N2a 0.0097, and so on.
- A power-iteration script gives exactly these numbers.

**Real model.**
- Total = 1.7332.
- Background 0.538.
- Each of Abf1's 28 states: 5.19e-8, all equal.
- Nucleosome:
  - each single state: 0.000783;
  - each 4-state position: split unevenly (position 1 = 0.310 / 0.184 / 0.190 / 0.316 of 0.000783), summing to 0.000783.
- Totals: background 54%, TFs 35%, nucleosome 11.5%.

**Checks.**
- The agent's independent power iteration matches the driver to 5.5e-9.
- The same vector is used for every read.

**Versus the old motif-first start** (still used by aggregate decodes):
- Only 310 states can start: background, each TF strand's first base, and the nucleosome's first state.
- A read could never begin inside a TF site or nucleosome.
- Background 93%, nucleosome 0.14%.

**Your earlier description** ("every base of a feature gets the first base's value, then normalise") is exact for background and TFs. For the nucleosome, the value is split across the 4 states at each middle position, not copied to each.

## 5. End anywhere, and the end vector in the forward pass
Sources: `/usr/project/xtmp/nd141/startend_check/end/`; `pkg/robocop/algo_log.c`.

**Backward pass.** End-anywhere = every state gets 1 at the last base (`algo_log.c:197-198`). That is all it takes, as you said. A brute-force toy confirms it equals "this read is a window cut from a longer chain" (difference ≤ 4e-16). Reads can end mid-feature, which is intended.

**Forward pass.** The code *also* multiplies the end vector into the last forward row (`algo_log.c:148-149`; `algo.c:111` upstream).

In a standard HMM with end probabilities e_j, you put e_j in **one** place:
- either the likelihood is Σ_j f_T(j)·e_j (forward), with backward started at b_T(j) = 1;
- or the backward is started at b_T(j) = e_j and the forward is left alone.

RoboCOP does both. The posterior at the last base is ∝ f_T(j)·b_T(j), so a non-uniform e_j would enter squared. A test with random end weights confirmed this.

**Verdict:** not correct in general, but harmless here. With end-anywhere, e_j = 1, log 1 = 0, and the extra term does nothing (log-likelihood matches a plain forward–backward to 3e-13). If a real end vector is ever used, remove it from one pass. This is on the revisit list.
